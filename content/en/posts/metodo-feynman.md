# What is the Feynman method?

Richard Feynman was a physicist with a very simple rule: ==if you can't explain it in simple words, you don't understand it==. The method named after him turns that idea into **four steps**.

> [!FEYNMAN]
> Learning isn't memorizing strange words. It's being able to tell the idea to a 12-year-old and have them get it the first time.

## The four steps

1. **Pick a concept** and write its name at the top of a page.
2. **Explain it to a kid**: no jargon, short sentences.
3. **Find the gaps**: wherever you get stuck, go back and study.
4. **Simplify and use analogies**: "it's like…".

```pasos
# put the 4 steps of the method in order
Pick a concept
Explain it like to a kid
Find your gaps and study again
Simplify and use analogies
```

## Why does it work?

When you explain, your brain has to *organize* the information. If something is shaky, you notice right away: you go quiet in the middle of a sentence. That pause is a **gift**, because it tells you exactly what to review.

- Explaining forces you to organize ideas
- The gaps show up on their own
  - and you know what to study next
  - without wasting time on what you already know
- Analogies make you remember

### An analogy with cookies

Imagine your knowledge is a **box of cookies**. Reading a book is putting cookies in the box. Explaining is *taking them out one by one*: if one is broken, you see it right away. 🍪

> What I cannot create, I do not understand.
> — Richard Feynman
